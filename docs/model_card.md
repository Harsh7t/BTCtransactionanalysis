# Model card — BTC-FUSION detector

**Version** artifacts/bulk · feature version 7 · seed 20260826 · 141 features ·
backend `sklearn_histgb` · fusion weights 1.00 / 0.00 / 0.00 (ranking is the classifier's
job alone; see technical_writeup.md §4)

## Intended use

Generating **prioritised, explainable investigative leads** for a human analyst on an
air-gapped workstation. The output is a ranked queue of case files, each carrying its
evidence, its reasoning, and its confidence.

## Out-of-scope use

- **This is not evidence.** An alert is a hypothesis with a confidence attached. It is a
  starting point for an investigation, never a finding.
- **Not for automated action.** Nothing here should freeze an account, block a
  transaction, or be actioned without a human reading the case file.
- **Not a real-world detection rate.** Every figure is measured on synthetic data we
  generated. See `docs/generator_parameters.md`.
- **Not attribution-grade identification.** A network attribution at confidence 0.91 is a
  lead about an *address*, not an identification of a person.

## Training data

Synthetic, generated by `btcfusion/generator/` from a fixed seed. 8 actor archetypes,
6 laundering typologies (2 held out entirely). Entity labels are recovered by mapping
resolved clusters to ground truth by majority address, at mean purity 0.9956 (bulk).

Splits are entity-grouped and temporally forward; calibration uses its own fold. All three
rules are enforced by assertions that raise, not warn.

## Evaluation

Reported on the **bulk** profile (2,467,299 rows), test fold n=59,043, 232 positives,
base rate 0.0039 — the realistic one. The demo profile's 3.3% positive rate produces
materially better figures and should not be quoted as the system's performance.

| PR-AUC of the ranking the analyst is shown | **0.2998** (77× base rate) |
| PR-AUC of the calibrated probability | 0.2803 |
| Precision @ 10 / 25 / 50, shipped queue | 1.00 / 0.96 / 0.92 — **P@10 has a wide CI, ≈±0.30; prefer P@50** |
| ECE | 0.0381 |

**Accuracy is 0.9953 against an all-negative baseline of 0.9961** — by accuracy the model
is worse than doing nothing. Accuracy is not used to judge this system.

## Known failure modes

From the measured failure gallery in `metrics.json`:

- **Legitimate pass-through wallets flagged as mules.** Short-lived wallets that receive
  one chunk and forward it are structurally near-identical to laundering mules.
  Separating them needs counterparty reputation this system deliberately does not model.
- **Shared infrastructure lifts the network-layer features.** An entity announced almost
  entirely from VPN, Tor or hosting addresses can score high without the chain layer
  supporting the flag.
- **Actors with little history are missed.** Genuine `rapid_layering` and extortion actors
  with only a handful of transactions score near zero: there is not enough activity to show
  a topology. (Bulk gallery: ENT-31606, ENT-325798, ENT-25041.)
- **Attribution inverts below ~10% observation coverage** — worse than guessing.

## Ethical considerations

This is a surveillance tool built for a signals-intelligence context. Three design choices
follow from that:

- **Abstention is a feature.** Where every candidate address is shared infrastructure the
  engine suppresses attribution rather than reporting a misleading number. 624 abstentions
  in the bulk evaluation (195 on demo).
- **Victims are never labelled illicit.** Being paid by a criminal does not make you one,
  and a generator that labelled payers illicit would teach the model to flag bystanders.
- **Analyst feedback re-ranks presentation only.** Verdicts demote or promote the next
  run's queue; they never retrain the model. Retraining on analyst clicks without a
  controlled evaluation is how a triage tool quietly learns one person's habits.

## Known behaviour: score quality depends on how much of the entity was observed

This model scores an entity from aggregates over whatever the capture contains. A capture
ends, so an entity first seen shortly before that end is scored from a truncated window,
and the score is correspondingly weaker. This is measured rather than assumed
(`artifacts/*/metrics.json → results.test.censoring`; the day ranges are the demo quartile edges):

| observation-window quartile | demo ROC-AUC | bulk ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|
| shortest (demo: under 7.4 days) | 0.761 | 0.559 |
| second (7.4 – 13.3 days) | 0.865 | 0.664 |
| third (13.3 – 18.5 days) | 0.911 | 0.727 |
| longest (over 18.5 days) | 0.938 | 0.767 |

Five features normalise the aggregates by the available window and recover part of the
gap, but nothing creates evidence that was never recorded.

**Operational consequence.** A low score on a recently-observed entity is weak evidence of
innocence, not strong evidence. Entities that never sent a transaction score at **0.490** on demo and **0.497** on bulk —
indistinguishable from chance, because nearly every feature is sender-side — and should be
read as *unranked*, never as cleared. When quoting a headline figure for this model, quote
the stratified table, not the mean over a fold that mixes fully-observed and
barely-observed entities.
